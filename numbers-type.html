Polish Grammar Guide: Cardinal vs. Ordinal Numbers

A simple breakdown of when to use cardinal numbers (liczebniki główne) versus ordinal numbers (liczebniki porządkowe) in Polish, with examples.

The Two Families of Numbers

Polish numbers split into two groups. Cardinal numbers (liczebniki główne) answer the question Ile? (How many?) and tell you a quantity. Ordinal numbers (liczebniki porządkowe) answer Który? / Która? / Które? (Which one in order?) and tell you a position in a sequence.

The key difference: cardinals count things, ordinals rank them. Ordinals behave like adjectives, so they change their ending to agree with the gender, number, and case of the noun they describe.

1. Cardinal Numbers (Liczebniki Główne)

Use cardinals when you are stating a plain quantity: how many objects, people, or units there are.

When to use Cardinals Ile? / How many?
Situation Polish Example English
Counting objects Mam trzy koty. I have three cats.
Counting people W pokoju jest pięć osób. There are five people in the room.
Prices and amounts To kosztuje dwadzieścia złotych. It costs twenty złoty.
Age Mam trzydzieści lat. I am thirty years old.
Phone numbers, math, plain counting Dwa plus dwa równa się cztery. Two plus two equals four.
Note: The number jeden / jedna / jedno (one) and the number dwa / dwie (two) already change for gender even as cardinals. Most higher cardinals (pięć, sześć...) stay fixed but force the counted noun into the genitive plural: pięć kotów (five cats).

2. Ordinal Numbers (Liczebniki Porządkowe)

Use ordinals when you place something in an order or sequence: first, second, third, and so on. Because they act like adjectives, their endings agree with the noun.

When to use Ordinals Który? / Which one?
Situation Polish Example English
Position in a sequence To jest mój pierwszy dzień. This is my first day.
Floors / storeys Mieszkam na trzecim piętrze. I live on the third floor.
Dates (day of the month) Dziś jest drugi maja. Today is the second of May.
Order of events / placings Zajął pierwsze miejsce. He took first place.
Telling the hour (o'clock) Jest piąta godzina. It is the fifth hour (five o'clock).
Note: Ordinals change ending to match gender: pierwszy dzień (m), pierwsza klasa (f), pierwsze miejsce (n). They also decline through the cases, just like regular adjectives: na trzecim piętrze (locative).

3. Quick Comparison

The same base number looks different depending on whether you are counting or ordering.

Number Cardinal (quantity) Ordinal (position)
1 jeden — Mam jeden bilet. (I have one ticket.) pierwszy — To jest pierwszy bilet. (This is the first ticket.)
2 dwa — Mam dwa psy. (I have two dogs.) drugi — To mój drugi pies. (This is my second dog.)
5 pięć — Jest pięć jabłek. (There are five apples.) piąty — To piąty dzień. (This is the fifth day.)

Quick Tip

  • Answering Ile? (How many?) → use a cardinal.
  • Answering Który / Która / Które? (Which in order?) → use an ordinal.
  • Ordinals are adjectives: match the gender, number, and case of their noun.
